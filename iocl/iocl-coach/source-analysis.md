## 4. Recurring micro-topics (the highest-value facts to drill)

Derived from the 400 source questions. These recur across **multiple independent sets**, which is the only repeat-evidence available to us — a topic appearing in 3 of 4 papers is the closest thing to a real signal here.

**Machines:** transformer OC/SC test + efficiency/regulation · induction motor slip & rotor frequency · synchronous speed `N=120f/P` · DC shunt back-emf · DC series flux∝current speed behaviour · transformer EMF `E=4.44fNΦ`.

**Power Systems:** per-unit conversion (`Z_pu = Z·MVA_b/kV_b²`) · symmetrical components · fault current by type (3-φ vs SLG vs LL) · Gauss-Seidel / Newton-Raphson bus types · equal-area criterion · distance relay reach · Ferranti effect · voltage regulation.

**Power Electronics:** controlled-rectifier average output (`(Vm/π)(1+cosα)` etc.) · chopper duty cycle (buck `D·Vs`, boost `Vs/(1−D)`) · inverter/PWM harmonics & THD · IGBT vs MOSFET trade-off · switching loss · diode reverse recovery.

**EM Fields & Materials:** Gauss's law & `∇·E = ρ/ε₀` · `∇×E = 0` (conservative) · B at loop centre `μ₀I/2a` · B from long conductor `μ₀I/2πr` · force between parallel conductors · reluctance/MMF/magnetic circuits · capacitor energy `½CV²` · inductance `μ₀μrN²A/l`.

**Network Theory:** Thevenin/Norton source-zeroing rules · max power transfer (`RL=Rth`, AC conjugate) · series resonance `X_L=X_C`, `Q`, bandwidth · parallel resonance (max impedance) · two-port Z/Y parameters · `RL`/`RC` time constants · RMS/form factor for standard waveforms.

**Control:** Routh first-column positivity · Nyquist `Z = N + P` · error constants `Kp/Kv/Ka` and `e_ss` by system type · `%Mp = exp(−πζ/√(1−ζ²))` · lead vs lag compensator form · root-locus start = open-loop poles.

**Signals:** Nyquist rate & **aliasing calculation** (`f_alias = |f − n·f_s|`) · Laplace ROC (right of pole, causal) · Z-transform of `aⁿu[n]` · Fourier symmetry (half-wave → odd harmonics only) · final value theorem.

**Measurements:** Wheatstone balance `R₁R₃ = R₂Rₓ` · potentiometer constant (V/cm) · CT burden `I²R` · ADC resolution / quantization step / quantization noise · CRO timebase & Lissajous · Schering (capacitance) vs Maxwell (inductance) vs Wien (frequency).

**Analog & Digital:** non-inverting gain `1+Rf/R₁` · slew rate `SR = 2πfV_m` · Sallen-Key `f_c = 1/(2πRC)` · DAC/ADC LSB · flip-flop/counter behaviour · CMOS dynamic power · 555/PLL basics.

## 5. Question style (what every drill must replicate)

Measured across the 400 sources:

- **~40% single-concept recall** — "which theorem", "what does X measure", "the unit of Y". One-line answers. Cheapest marks on the paper.
- **~35% one-formula numeric** — plug into a known formula, pick the closest option. No multi-stage derivation anywhere in the sources.
- **~20% conceptual discriminator** — two nearly-identical options where only one is correct (`s/f` vs `s·f`; `μ₀I/2a` vs `μ₀I/2πa`). This is where the paper separates candidates.
- **~5% broken or ambiguous** — stems with missing data or option sets containing no correct answer. **Confirmed by direct inspection of source Set 02 Q17 and Q18**, where no option carries the correct value. So this is a property of the *sources*, and the repair pass flags these rather than inventing fixes.

**What to replicate in every drill:**
1. Fire **one question at a time**. No walls of text.
2. Wrong options are the **exact inverse**, the **1-φ vs 3-φ version**, the **unit in a different system**, or the **right number with a decimal shift**.
3. No derivation in the question stem — the source papers never ask for one.
4. Numeric answers are "closest to" as often as exact.

## 6. What this means for the engine

1. **Do not teach derivations by default.** The sources contain no multi-step derivations. Coaching default is fact → why → trap → move. The AEE 5-step method runs **only** on an explicit `deep <topic>`.
2. **Breadth beats depth.** With 18 flat topics and no reliable weightage, skipping a block is the highest-risk strategy available. Every block gets time.
3. **Drill the discriminator traps hardest.** The ~20% conceptual-discriminator band is where marks are actually won; the recall band is won by showing up.
4. **Exclude the flagged questions from scoring.** `[BROKEN SOURCE]` / `[AMBIGUOUS STEM]` items train you against wrong answers — they appear only in `iocl setter-hunt` mode.
5. **Confirm the pattern before trusting the clock.** If negative marking is real, guessing is a losing strategy and the per-question budget changes. Until verified, use the conservative rule in §1.1.
6. **Never claim official weightage in-session.** If asked "how many marks is Machines?", the honest answer is: *unknown; the sources are institute sets and are flat; we cover everything.*
