### Q17–Q21. Rearrangement (AI & Robotics)

**Sentence set (as printed in the PDF):**
(A) Artificial intelligence and robotics are very recent technologies and risks for our world.
(B) They may also inherit the full nature of creatures.
(C) When humans see the past histories of AI and robotics, human beings can examine and understand the objectives and intentions of them to make life easy and assist human beings within different circumstances and situations.
(D) However, currently and in the near future, due to the change in the attitude of robotic and AI inventors and experts as well as based on the AI nature that their capacity of environmental acquisition and adaptation, they may become predators and put creatures at risk.
(E) They are developing their capacity dramatically and shifting their origins of developing intention to other dimensions.
(F) Thus, finally they will create their new universe or the destiny of our universe will be in danger.

**Correct order: A → E → C → D → B → F**, justified by the connectors:

- **A opens the paragraph.** It is the only sentence that introduces the subject ("Artificial intelligence and robotics"); every other sentence leans on it — E, D and B all begin with "They" and have no antecedent before A.
- **A → E.** "They" in E = "Artificial intelligence and robotics" of A; E continues the same subject ("developing their capacity dramatically").
- **E → C.** C shifts to the human response ("When humans see the past histories of AI and robotics … to make life easy and assist human beings") — the optimistic note that D then cancels. C must precede D, not follow it.
- **C → D.** D opens with "However", which needs the positive statement C ("make life easy and assist human beings") to contrast against; D also supplies "creatures", which B repeats.
- **D → B.** "They may **also** inherit the full nature of creatures" — "also" piles onto D's list of threats ("they may become predators and put creatures at risk"), and "creatures" is a direct repeat from D.
- **B → F.** "**Thus, finally** they will create their new universe or the destiny of our universe will be in danger" is the conclusion, and it can only follow the accumulation of threats in D–B ("Thus" + "finally").

The rejected rival order (A → E → D → B → C → F) leaves "However" contrasting with the neutral sentence E, and leaves "Thus, finally" hanging off C's general statement instead of off the threat sequence D–B.

**Q17. THIRD sentence:** (e) C
**Q18. FIRST sentence:** (a) A
**Q19. FIFTH sentence:** (c) B
**Q20. SECOND sentence:** (b) E
**Q21. LAST sentence:** (e) F

> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** A→E→C→D→B→F is the only order in which every connector resolves: "They" (E, D, B) needs A, "However" (D) needs the optimistic C, "also … creatures" (B) needs D, and "Thus, finally" (F) must close the threat sequence. All five answers hit a printed option: Q17 = C (e), Q18 = A (a), Q19 = B (c), Q20 = E (b), Q21 = F (e).

### Q71–Q73. Syllogism

Convention used throughout: "Only a few A are B" gives two certainties — **Some A are B** and **Some A are not B** — and nothing else.

**Q71. Statements:** All Gate are Park. Only a few Park are Road. All Road are Subway.
**Conclusions:** I. No Gate is Subway. II. Some Park are not Road.

- II: "Only a few Park are Road" → Some Park are Road **and** Some Park are not Road ✓ definite.
- I: Gate ⊆ Park, and Road ⊆ Subway, but only *some* Park are Road. A model in which some Gate sit in the Park∩Road part makes those Gate Subway, so "No Gate is Subway" is not forced.
**Answer: (b) If only II follows**
> **[VERIFIED]** only conclusion II is certain = printed option (b) exactly.

**Q72. Statements:** No Soap is Pen. Only a few Soap is Eraser. Only a few Eraser is Clip.
**Conclusions:** I. Some Eraser are Pen. II. Some Clip is not Soap.

- I: counter-model — put every Eraser inside Soap. Then Eraser ∩ Pen = ∅ (since Soap ∩ Pen = ∅), which satisfies all three statements, yet no Eraser is Pen. Not definite.
- II: counter-model — put every Clip inside the Soap∩Eraser overlap. Then every Clip is a Soap (satisfies "some Soap are Eraser" and "some Eraser are Clip"), so "Some Clip is not Soap" fails. Not definite.
- They are also not complementary (their terms differ), so "either follows" does not apply.
**Answer: (c) If neither I nor II follows**
> **[VERIFIED]** both conclusions have consistent counter-models = printed option (c). ("Only a few Eraser is Clip" says some Eraser are Clip and some Eraser are not Clip; it places no Clip *outside* Soap.)

**Q73. Statements:** Some Giraffe is Kangaroo. All Turtle is Giraffe. All Seal is Kangaroo.
**Conclusions:** I. Some Turtle is Kangaroo. II. All Giraffe can be Seal.

- I: Turtle ⊆ Giraffe, but the Giraffes that are Kangaroo need not be the Turtles → not definite.
- II: consistent model — "Some Giraffe is Kangaroo" does not forbid *all* Giraffe being Kangaroo, and Seal ⊆ Kangaroo does not forbid Kangaroo ⊆ Seal, so Giraffe ⊆ Seal ⊆ Kangaroo (with Turtle inside Giraffe) satisfies everything → the possibility holds.
**Answer: (b) If only II follows**
> **[VERIFIED]** only the possibility conclusion II holds = printed option (b) exactly.

### Q84–Q88. Month & State Puzzle

**Given:**
- 6 persons: R, T, P, U, S, Q.
- States: Delhi, Sikkim, Odisha, Goa, Kerala, Assam.
- Dates: 9th, 26th of May, June, July.
- One person between P and Delhi.
- Delhi not in even month or even date.
- Delhi and Goa not first.
- Two between T and U (Goa).
- T after P.
- Odisha just before S.
- Two between Kerala and Assam.
- U not just after Assam.
- Between Q and S = one more than before R.

**Slots:** 1 = May 9, 2 = May 26, 3 = June 9, 4 = June 26, 5 = July 9, 6 = July 26.

**Step-by-step deduction:**
1. Delhi is not in a month with an even number of days (that rules out June) and not on an even date (rules out the 26th) → Delhi is May 9 or July 9 (slot 1 or 5). Delhi is "not the first person to travel" → **Delhi = slot 5 (July 9)**.
2. One person travels between P and Delhi → |pos(P) − 5| = 2 → **P = slot 3 (June 9)**.
3. T travels after P, so T ∈ {4, 5, 6}. Two persons travel between T and U (who is Goa, and Goa is not first) → |pos(T) − pos(U)| = 3. The only pair satisfying this is **T = slot 5 (July 9, so T is the Delhi traveller) and U = slot 2 (May 26, Goa)**.
4. Odisha travels just before S. With slots 2, 3, 5 taken, S can only be slot 4 (Odisha's traveller sits at slot 3 = P) — S = 1 needs Odisha at slot 0, S = 6 needs Odisha at slot 5 which is T. → **P = Odisha (slot 3), S = slot 4 (June 26)**.
5. Two persons travel between Kerala and Assam → those two states are 3 slots apart. The remaining free slots are 1, 4 and 6, so Kerala/Assam occupy slots 1 and 4, and Sikkim falls to slot 6.
6. U (slot 2) does not travel just after Assam → Assam is not at slot 1 → **Assam = slot 4 (S)**, **Kerala = slot 1**.
7. "Persons between Q and S is one more than persons before R": R and Q fill slots 1 and 6. With **R = slot 1 (0 before him)** we need exactly 1 person between Q and S → Q = slot 6, S = slot 4, one person (T) between them ✓. With R = slot 6 (5 before him) the relation fails (only 2 people between Q = 1 and S = 4). → **R = slot 1 (Kerala), Q = slot 6 (July 26, Sikkim)**.

**Final arrangement:**

| Slot | Person | State |
|---|---|---|
| May 9 | R | Kerala |
| May 26 | U | Goa |
| June 9 | P | Odisha |
| June 26 | S | Assam |
| July 9 | T | Delhi |
| July 26 | Q | Sikkim |

Constraint check: P↔Delhi one between (P = 3, Delhi = 5) ✓; Delhi = July 9, odd month and odd date ✓; neither Delhi nor Goa is first ✓; T (5) and U (2) have two persons between ✓; T after P ✓; Odisha (3) just before S (4) ✓; Kerala (1) and Assam (4) have two persons between ✓; U (2) is not just after Assam (4) ✓; one person between Q (6) and S (4), and zero before R (1) → 1 = 0 + 1 ✓. The arrangement is unique.
