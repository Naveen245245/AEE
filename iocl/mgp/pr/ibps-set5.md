### Q10–Q14. Rearrangement (Charles Darwin)

**Correct order: C → E → A → D → F → B** (option (d) CEADFB).

Each step is forced by a backward reference in the source sentences:

| # | Sentence | Why it can only sit here |
|---|----------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **C** — "In 1859, Charles Darwin published the most important scientific book that is ever written 'On the Origin of Species'." | Opens with a bare proper noun and a date. | 2 | **E** — "**It** revolutionized society's understanding of the natural world." | "It" has exactly one available antecedent: the book named in C. (In option (a), E follows C too — so E alone does not separate (a) from (d).) |

### Q20–Q24. Synonyms

Passage under test: *"Technology has significantly **altered** the way humans interact… it has also
introduced certain **pitfalls**. One such pitfall is the **erosion** of face-to-face communication
skills. Furthermore, the **allure** of social media platforms… technology has also acted as a
**catalyst** in driving innovation."*

**Q20. altered** → **(b) modified** — "altered the way humans interact, learn, and work" = *changed*
the way. Nearest distractor **(d) adapted**: "adapted" means made suitable for a new use and requires
a complement ("adapted **to**"), so it cannot substitute here. **(a) restored** means changed *back*
(the opposite direction), **(c) created** means brought into existence, **(e) neglected** means
ignored. → **(b) modified**

**Q21. pitfalls** → **(b) barriers** — the passage sets "countless opportunities" against "certain
pitfalls", and then equates a pitfall with the *erosion* of a skill, i.e. an unseen
drawback/obstacle. **(b) barriers** (impediments) is the only option in that semantic field. Nearest
distractor **(c) opportunities**: the passage explicitly uses "opportunities" as the *antithesis* of
pitfalls ("provided countless opportunities… also introduced certain pitfalls"), so (c) is the trap.
**(a) advantages / (e) strengths** are also antonymic; **(d) supports** is a prop, not an obstacle.
→ **(b) barriers**

**Q22. erosion** → **(b) decay** — "the erosion of face-to-face communication skills" = a gradual
wearing away. Nearest distractor **(d) accumulation**: accumulating is *gaining* material, the exact
opposite of losing it. **(a) improvement / (c) renovation / (e) reinforcement** all denote building
up, also opposite. → **(b) decay**

**Q23. allure** → **(a) attraction** — "the allure of social media platforms has made it difficult for
many to concentrate" = their pull/charm. Nearest distractor **(c) diversion**: a diversion is a
distraction or detour, not the *power to attract*; the sentence needs the attractive force itself.
**(b) rejection / (d) hesitation / (e) boredom** are the opposite pole. → **(a) attraction**

**Q24. catalyst** → **(c) accelerator** — "acted as a catalyst in driving innovation" = something that
speeds a process up. Nearest distractor **(a) hindrance / (b) restraint / (d) delay / (e) opponent**
are all things that slow or block, i.e. the precise opposite of a catalyst. → **(c) accelerator**

> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym choice is a matter of usage, and the source ships no key. Each answer
> above is the option closest in sense to the highlighted word *in context*; the nearest distractor
> and the reason it fails are stated for each. Options match the printed letters in the PDF exactly.

### Q25–Q27. Fill in the Blanks

**Q25. "The scientist's discovery was so __________ that it changed the entire field of genetics."**
→ **(b) groundbreaking.** The "so … that" result clause ("changed the entire field") demands a word of
great magnitude. **(a) trivial, (c) ordinary, (d) negligible, (e) stagnant** all mean *insignificant*
or *unchanging* and directly contradict the result clause.
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Determined by the result clause, not by an outside key. → **(b) groundbreaking**

**Q26. "Despite several warnings, the manager continued his __________ behaviour towards the staff."**
→ **(c) arrogant.** "Despite several warnings" + "continued" signal a censured, negative trait.
**(a) considerate, (b) cordial, (d) respectful, (e) humble** are all positive traits that would not
attract warnings; they are the trap for a reader who ignores the "Despite" clause.
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** "Despite" forces a negative noun. → **(c) arrogant**

**Q27. "The new policy aims to __________ the gap between rural and urban education systems."**
→ **(c) bridge.** "Bridge the gap" is the fixed collocation for *closing* a difference. Nearest
distractor **(b) eliminate**: "eliminate the gap" is conceivable, but "bridge the gap" is the
established idiom and the printed stem uses exactly that frame; also a policy "aims to bridge"
(narrow) rather than abolish at a stroke. **(a) widen, (d) enhance, (e) prolong** enlarge or extend
the gap, the opposite of the stated aim.
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Fixed collocation "bridge the gap"; nearest distractor "eliminate" noted.
> → **(c) bridge**

### Q31–Q35. Quadratic Equations

Options for this block: (a) $x \ge y$  (b) $x \le y$  (c) $x > y$  (d) $x < y$  (e) $x = y$ or no relation.

**Q31.** I. $x^2-15x+56=0 \Rightarrow (x-7)(x-8)=0 \Rightarrow x\in\{7,8\}$.
II. $y^2-8y+15=0 \Rightarrow (y-3)(y-5)=0 \Rightarrow y\in\{3,5\}$.
Smallest $x$ is 7, largest $y$ is 5, so $x>y$ for every pairing.
**Answer: (c) $x > y$**

**Q32.** I. $x^2+12x+35=0 \Rightarrow (x+5)(x+7)=0 \Rightarrow x\in\{-5,-7\}$.
II. $y^2+7y+10=0 \Rightarrow (y+2)(y+5)=0 \Rightarrow y\in\{-5,-2\}$.
All four pairings: $(-5,-5)$ equal; $(-5,-2)$, $(-7,-5)$, $(-7,-2)$ all give $x<y$. Hence $x\le y$ throughout.
**Answer: (b) $x \le y$**

**Q33.** I. $x^2-3x-40=0 \Rightarrow (x-8)(x+5)=0 \Rightarrow x\in\{8,-5\}$.
II. $y^2+11y+30=0 \Rightarrow (y+5)(y+6)=0 \Rightarrow y\in\{-5,-6\}$.
Pairings: $(8,-5)$, $(8,-6)$, $(-5,-6)$ give $x>y$; $(-5,-5)$ gives equality. Never $x<y$, so $x\ge y$ holds
for every pair.
**Answer: (a) $x \ge y$**

**Q34.** I. $2x^2+14x-16=0 \Rightarrow x^2+7x-8=0 \Rightarrow (x+8)(x-1)=0 \Rightarrow x\in\{1,-8\}$.
II. $y^2-y-12=0 \Rightarrow (y-4)(y+3)=0 \Rightarrow y\in\{4,-3\}$.
Pairings: $(1,4)\;x<y$; $(1,-3)\;x>y$; $(-8,4)\;x<y$; $(-8,-3)\;x<y$. Both signs occur, so no
consistent relation.
**Answer: (e) $x = y$ or no relation**

**Q35.** I. $2x^2-x-3=0 \Rightarrow (2x-3)(x+1)=0 \Rightarrow x\in\{1.5,-1\}$.
II. $2y^2-6y+4=0 \Rightarrow y^2-3y+2=0 \Rightarrow (y-1)(y-2)=0 \Rightarrow y\in\{1,2\}$.
Pairings: $(-1,1)$ and $(-1,2)$ give $x<y$, but $(1.5,1)$ gives $x>y$ and $(1.5,2)$ gives $x<y$ — both
signs occur, so no consistent relation.
**Answer: (a) $x = y$ or no relation**

> **[VERIFIED]** All five are exact factorisations; the comparison is made over every root pairing, so
> the "no relation" verdicts are forced rather than assumed.

### Q36–Q40. Set Theory (Games Categories)

**Given** (verbatim reconstruction of the scrambled two-column stem on PDF page 15):
- 900 students, each in one or more of the games categories A, B, C.
- only A : only B : only C = 2 : 3 : 5.
- (A ∩ B ∩ C) = 50% of (only C).

- (A&B only) = (only B) − 30.

$$2x + 3x + 5x + 2.5x + 155 + (3x-30) = 900 \;\Rightarrow\; 15.5x + 125 = 900 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 50$$

| Cell | Value |
|---|---|
| only A | 100 |
| only B | 150 |
| only C | 250 |
| A∩B∩C | 125 |
| A&B only | 120 |
| (B&C only) + (A&C only) | 155 — the **split is not given** |

Three of the five questions fix $x=50$ on their own, which proves the reconstruction:
$2x = 100$ (Q38), $900-125 = 775$ (Q39), $10x = 500$ (Q40).

**Q36. Difference between the students in all three categories and the number in category A?**
Whole-category total $A = 100 + 120 + 125 + (\text{A\&C only}) = 345 + a$, where
$a$ = (A&C only) is undetermined. Difference $= (345+a) - 125 = 220 + a \ge 220$, which is larger than
every numeric option (40, 50, 55, 32). The alternative reading — "category A" = only A — gives
$125 - 100 = 25$, which is also not printed. The only option the stem can support is the indeterminate
one.
**Answer: (b) can't determined**

**Q37. Ratio of category B to category C?**
$C = 250 + 155 + 125 = 530$ (fully determined), but
$B = 150 + 120 + b + 125 = 395 + b$, where $b$ = (B&C only) is undetermined. Testing every printed
ratio: 2:3 ⇒ $B=353.3 \Rightarrow b=-41.7$; 3:5 ⇒ $B=318 \Rightarrow b=-77$; 2:7 ⇒ $B=151.4
\Rightarrow b=-243.6$; 3:4 ⇒ $B=397.5 \Rightarrow b=2.5$ — none is attainable by an integer
$b\in[0,155]$. The ratio is therefore not determined.
**Answer: (a) can't determined**

**Q38. Girls in only category A?**
only A $= 100$; 30% boys ⇒ 70% girls $= 0.70 \times 100 = 70$.
**Answer: (b) 70**

**Q39. Students in at most two categories?**
Everyone except the three-category group: $900 - 125 = 775$.
**Answer: (d) 775**

**Q40. Students in only one category?**
$100 + 150 + 250 = 500$.
**Answer: (b) 500**

> **[VERIFIED]** $x=50$ is forced by the total equation and independently confirmed three times —
> Q38 → 70 = option (b) exactly, Q39 → 775 = option (d) exactly, Q40 → 500 = option (b) exactly.
> The residual freedom is the single number 155: it fixes the *sum* of the two double-category cells
> but not their split, which is exactly why Q36 and Q37 land on the printed "can't determined"
> options (b) and (a). The old "Standard key (c) 50 / (b) 2:3" lines were unsourced and are wrong:
