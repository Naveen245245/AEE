### Q19–Q23. Error Spotting

**Q19.** (a) The Reserve Bank of India on Friday (b) announced that it has extended the deadline (c) for banks to complete (d) the recurring payments through cards by six months (e) No error
"announced" is past tense and the extension preceded the announcement, so the reported clause needs the past perfect: "announced that it **had extended** the deadline". Part (d) is acceptable as written — "extended the deadline … **by** six months" is the correct idiom for the length of an extension.
**Answer: (b)**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Sequence of tenses ("has extended" → "had extended"). The nearest trap is (d): it reads oddly but is not ungrammatical, whereas (b) is a definite tense error.

**Q20.** (a) Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Thursday (b) launch a platform to honour the nation's teachers (c) on the occasion of Teachers' Day (d) and said teachers play a vital role in nation-building (e) No error
The time marker "on Thursday" and the coordinated verb "said" require the past tense: "(he) **launched** a platform".
**Answer: (b)**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Subject–verb tense: finite verb "launch" cannot follow a past-time adverbial and coordinate with "said".

**Q21.** (a) In view of the COVID-19 pandemic, (b) the Supreme Court on Friday (c) extend the limitation period (d) for filing cases in courts across the country (e) No error
Same past-tense requirement: "the Supreme Court on Friday **extended** the limitation period".
**Answer: (c)**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Bare-infinitive form "extend" used as the main verb after a past-time adverbial; the finite past form is required. (a), (b) and (d) are all well formed.

**Q22.** (a) India on Friday (b) rejected China's allegation (c) that Indian troops had illegally transgressed (d) the Line of Actual Control in Ladakh (e) No error
"had illegally transgressed" correctly marks the action as earlier than "rejected", and "transgress" is transitive, so the bare object "the Line of Actual Control in Ladakh" is fine. Every part is grammatical.
**Answer: (e) No error**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Past perfect for the earlier of two past actions; no article/preposition is missing before "the Line of Actual Control".

**Q23.** (a) The Bombay High Court on Friday (b) said that the media should exercise restraint (c) while reporting on the investigation into actor Sushant Singh Rajput's death (d) and not hamper the ongoing probe (e) No error
"should exercise restraint … and (should) not hamper the ongoing probe" is a correct coordination with the shared modal ellipted; "reporting on the investigation into … death" is the right preposition set. No error.
**Answer: (e) No error**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** The only candidate, (d), is a coordinated verb phrase that inherits "should" from (b) — ellipsis of a repeated modal is standard, so no error can be pinned on any part.

### Q24–Q30. Word Replacement

**Q24.** "The government has announced a **stimulus** package to revive the economy."
The word to be replaced is the noun **stimulus** (the options are all nouns). A stimulus is something that rouses or quickens activity; a government "stimulus package" and a "relief package" are the same measure, and "relief" is the only option that keeps the sentence's positive sense. Burden, loss, neglect and decline would reverse the meaning.
**Answer: (a) Relief**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (stimulus ≈ relief/boost). Nearest distractor (e) "Decline" is the opposite of what a stimulus produces.

**Q25.** "The court has given a **verdict** in favour of the petitioner."
A verdict is the decision/judgment a court delivers, so "decision" substitutes without change of meaning. Delay, argument, appeal and plea name other courtroom things (a postponement, a submission, a challenge, a request) but none is a verdict.
**Answer: (a) Decision**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (verdict = decision/judgment). (d) "Appeal" and (e) "Plea" are the traps — both are things a party files, not what the court "gives in favour of" a party.

**Q26.** "The manager asked the staff to **adhere** to the safety guidelines."
"Adhere to" = follow/observe (a rule). Violate, break, ignore and avoid are all antonyms of adhere.
**Answer: (b) Follow**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (adhere to ≈ follow). The four distractors are the opposite action, so they would change the meaning of the sentence.

**Q27.** "The scientist presented a **hypothesis** for further research."
A hypothesis is a supposition put forward to be tested — i.e. an assumption taken as a starting point, which is exactly what "for further research" implies. Fact, proof and rule are wrong because a hypothesis is by definition not yet established.
**Answer: (a) Assumption**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Nearest distractor is (e) "Theory": a theory is an already-substantiated explanatory framework, whereas the sentence presents the idea *for further research*, i.e. still unproven — the sense of "assumption".

**Q28.** "The medicine had an **adverse** effect on the patient."
"Adverse" = unfavourable/harmful. Positive, helpful, curative and useful all describe a good effect, the reverse of adverse.
**Answer: (b) Harmful**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (adverse ≈ harmful). (d) "Curative" is the closest trap — it repeats the medical context but reverses the meaning.

**Q29.** "The soldier showed great **valour** in the battlefield."
Valour = courage, especially in battle. Fear, weakness, cowardice and panic are antonyms.
**Answer: (a) Courage**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (valour ≈ courage/bravery). (d) "Cowardice" is the direct antonym and the likeliest mis-read.

**Q30.** "The company is planning to **expand** its business overseas."
"Expand" = make larger, grow. Contract, reduce, shrink and lessen all mean to make smaller.
**Answer: (c) Grow**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** Synonym (expand ≈ grow). (d) "Shrink" is its exact opposite; (a) "Contract" is the business-context trap.

### Q66–Q70. Parallel Rows (14 persons)

**Given:**

Row 2 (South): C, D, R, S, M, N, Z

- Y diagonally opposite Z.
- One between Z and R.

- Three between B and Q.
- C diagonally opposite Q.
- C and D immediate neighbours.
- N faces K.

Seats are numbered 1–7 left to right as printed; row-1 seat *i* faces row-2 seat *i*. Row 1 faces north, so "right" = towards seat 7; row 2 faces south, so "right" = towards seat 1.

**Arrangement:**
Row 1: Q, A, P, L, B, Y, K
Row 2: D, C, R, M, Z, S, N
Facing pairs: Q–D, A–C, P–R, L–M, B–Z, Y–S, K–N.

(The nine clues admit one other solution — row 1 K, L, Q, A, P, Y, B with row 2 N, M, D, C, R, S, Z — but there L is at seat 2 and Q67 has no answer at all, so the arrangement above is the intended one; it is also the only one that works whichever way the ambiguous "to the right of M" clue is read.)

**Q66. Who faces S?**

**Answer: (e) None of these**
> **[VERIFIED]** The facing partner is Y, which is not among (a)–(d); (e) is the printed option for that case. Y faces S in every arrangement the clues allow.

**Q67. Who sits third to left of L?**
L is at row-1 seat 4. Row 1 faces north, so "left" = towards seat 1: 4 → 3 (P) → 2 (A) → 1 (Q).
**Answer: (a) Q**
> **[VERIFIED]** The third seat to L's left is Q = option (a) exactly.

**Q68. Position of M w.r.t. D?**
M is at row-2 seat 4 and D at row-2 seat 1. Row 2 faces south, so a person's left is towards the higher seat numbers; M is three seats to D's left.
**Answer: (c) Third to the left**
> **[VERIFIED]** Three seats = option (c) exactly. (Not "immediate left" — M and D are not neighbours.)

**Q69. Who sits fourth to right of one who faces Z?**
Z is at row-2 seat 5, so the person who faces Z is at row-1 seat 5 = B. Row 1 faces north, so the fourth seat to the right of seat 5 would be seat 9 — the row has only seven seats.
**Answer: — (no option derivable)**
> **[BROKEN SOURCE]** The person facing Z is B at seat 5; there is no fourth seat to B's right in a 7-seat row, so none of (a) Y / (b) P / (c) K / (d) L / (e) B can be derived. (The old file's "(c) K" had no derivation and contradicts its own arrangement.)

**Q70. Odd one out?**
(a) Z, (b) C, (c) Q, (d) R, (e) Y. Groupings tested on the arrangement above: by row — row 1: Q, Y against row 2: Z, C, R (3–2, not 4–1); by seat parity — odd: Q(1), R(3), Z(5) against even: C(2), Y(6) (3–2); by facing direction — the same 3–2 split. The only 4–1 reading available is "is diagonally opposite another member of this group": Z↔Y and C↔Q are diagonal pairs, while R's diagonal partners are A and L (outside the group).

> **[BROKEN SOURCE]** No grouping from row, facing or seat parity gives a 4–1 split. The "diagonally opposite a member of the group" reading isolates R (option d), but it is not robust — in the other arrangement the clues admit, all five members have a diagonal partner inside the group, so no odd one exists.
