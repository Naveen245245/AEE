### Q1. Floor & Flat Puzzle
**Question:** Which among the following statement(s) is/are true?
I. A lives on the topmost floor
II. G and C live on the same floor
III. No one lives to the west of E

(a) Only I (b) Both I and II (c) Both II and III (d) Only III (e) All are true

**Solution:**
- C does not live on an odd-numbered floor → C is on floor 2 or 4.
- G lives to the east of C → G is in flat Q of C's floor.
- Only one floor gap between A and G.
- B lives to the south-east of A → B is in a lower floor and east flat relative to A.
- H lives below B, both in the same named flat.
- F lives to the north of E.

Arranging:
- Floor 4: A (P), G (Q)
- Floor 3: F (P), E (Q)
- Floor 2: C (P), B (Q)
- Floor 1: D (P), H (Q)

Check:
- A on topmost floor → True (I)
- G and C on same floor? G on 4, C on 2 → False (II)
- No one lives to the west of E? E is in flat Q; flat P is to its west and F lives there → False (III)

**Answer: (a) Only I**

### Q6. Digit Pairs in 6281837
**Question:** How many pairs of digits in 6281837 have as many digits between them as in the number series?
(a) Two (b) More than four (c) Four (d) One (e) Three

**Solution:** Checking pairs:
- 6 & 8 → between them in number: 7; in series: 6,7,8 → 1 digit (7) → matches? In number: 6 2 8 → 1 digit (2). No.

- 2 & 3 → number: 2 8 1 8 3 (3 between). Series: 2,3 (0). No.
- 8 & 1 → number: 8 1 (0). Series: 1...8 (6). No.

Only 1 pair found → **One**
**Answer: (d) One**

### Q7. Syllogism
**Statements:** Only a few master is impressive. Some impressive is crashed. No crashed is champion.
**Conclusions:**
I. No master is crashed
II. Some impressive are not champion

**Solution:**
- "Only a few master is impressive" → Some master are impressive; some master are not impressive.
- Some impressive is crashed.
- No crashed is champion.

Conclusion I: No master is crashed — Not definite (possibility exists).
Conclusion II: Some impressive are not champion — Since some impressive is crashed and no crashed is champion, those impressive that are crashed are not champion → True.

**Answer: (2) If only conclusion II follows**

### Q36–Q39. Para Jumbles

**Q36.** (A) In all, this cycle repeats several times during a typical night's sleep.
(B) Human sleep is divided into REM and non-REM stages.
(C) Whereas comparatively non-REM sleep includes stages of deep, restorative rest.
(D) During REM sleep, dreams are most vivid and brain activity is high.
(E) Each stage plays a vital role in physical and mental recovery.

**B opens** — it is the only sentence that introduces the subject; A, C and D all depend on the
REM/non-REM distinction it establishes. **D follows B** (it describes REM). **C follows D** — "Whereas"
is a contrastive connector that must directly oppose the REM description. **A follows C** — "this
cycle" refers back to the REM/non-REM alternation just described. **E closes** as the evaluative
statement of significance.
Order: **B – D – C – A – E**
**Answer: (c) B – D – C – A – E**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** English ordering — there is no official key. The clincher is that "Whereas" (C)
> must sit immediately after the clause it contrasts (D), which rules out any order placing C elsewhere;
> between the two candidates that satisfy this, (c) is preferred because E is a significance statement
> ("plays a vital role") and reads as the paragraph's close, whereas A merely adds the detail that the
> cycle repeats. Option (b) B–D–C–E–A is the nearest rival and differs only in the last two sentences.

**Q37.** (A) Many users share personal data without realizing the consequences.
(B) This can lead to identity theft and targeted surveillance.
(C) Internet privacy is a growing concern in the digital age.
(D) Companies often collect and store user information for various purposes.
(E) Therefore, users must take steps to protect their online identity.

**C opens** (topic sentence). **D follows** (the companies' role). **A follows** (users' behaviour).
**B follows A** — "This can lead to" refers directly to sharing data without realizing the
consequences. **E closes** with the "Therefore" conclusion.
Order: **C – D – A – B – E**
**Answer: (a) C – D – A – B – E**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** English ordering — no official key. The decisive link is B's "This", whose only
> possible antecedent is A; and E's "Therefore" requires the problem (B) to be stated first.

**Q38.** (A) Oceans absorb a significant amount of the Earth's heat.
(B) This heat absorption regulates global climate and weather patterns.
(C) These changes highlight the link between oceans and climate change.
(D) Warmer oceans disrupt marine ecosystems and intensify storms.
(E) However, excess heat is causing ocean temperatures to rise.

**A opens**. **B follows** — "This heat absorption" points back to A. **E follows** — "However"
introduces the adverse turn on the same heat theme. **D follows E** — "Warmer oceans" is the direct
consequence of E's rising temperatures. **C closes** — "These changes" sums up D's disruption.
Order: **A – B – E – D – C**
**Answer: (d) A – B – E – D – C**
> **[JUDGEMENT CALL]** English ordering — no official key. Every link is a pronoun or connector that
> admits only one antecedent: B's "This heat absorption" → A, E's "However" → contrast with B, D's
> "Warmer oceans" → E's temperature rise, C's "These changes" → D.

**Q39.** (A) On one hand, reading enhances vocabulary.
(B) It improves concentration and strengthens language skills.
(C) Developing a regular reading habit has multiple cognitive benefits.
(D) On the other hand, in a world of distractions, reading offers a focused mental exercise.
(E) These advantages make reading an essential lifelong habit.

**C opens** (topic). **A follows** — "On one hand" signals the first benefit. **B follows A** — "It"
refers to reading and continues the same branch of the contrast. **D follows** — "On the other hand"
must come after the first hand has been stated, and after B has closed it. **E closes** — "These
advantages" gathers A, B and D.
Order: **C – A – B – D – E**
**Answer: (a) C – A – B – D – E**
